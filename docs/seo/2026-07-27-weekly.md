## Filed

**[#1: Two published post titles exceed the 65-char schema max — publish
validation was bypassed](https://github.com/jamesevans28/jamesevans.au/issues/1)**

The technical audit flagged all 5 new posts for titles over the 50–60 aim
(62–71 chars). Two of those — `ai-insurance-complaint-letter` (70 chars) and
`ai-not-relevant-to-my-business` (71 chars) — exceed not just the aim but
the **hard schema max of 65** (`src/lib/blog-schema.ts:28,44`), which
`publish`'s validation gate is supposed to enforce before any write
(`scripts/blog/index.ts:270-271`). `git log -p` on the schema file confirms
`TITLE_MAX` has always been 65 — it was never loosened — so these two posts
should have been rejected and weren't. That's a gap in the publish pipeline,
not a style call, so I filed it rather than fixing titles blind: shortening
a live post's title is a `blog-post`-voice decision, and the root cause
(how invalid data reached `published` status) is functional. Didn't touch
the three posts that are over the 50–60 aim but under the 65 hard max
(62–66 chars) — same reasoning as the 25 Jul run's decision to leave the
first post's title alone at 62 chars: trimming a live title for a handful
of characters risks reading worse for a benefit that's unmeasurable at this
traffic, and none of the three breach the actual constraint.
